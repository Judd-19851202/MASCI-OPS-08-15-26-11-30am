# FORGEDOPS · OPERATIONAL DESIGN CONSTITUTION

**Operator-issued doctrine** · 2026-06-02 · verbatim · ⛔ READ-BEFORE-TOUCHING · binds all future design / audit / build / scope / roadmap decisions until explicitly overridden by the operator.

---

## PART I · THE FRICTION RULE — CONSTITUTION

### Core Principle

ForgedOps exists to make construction operations easier, faster, clearer, and more accountable.

ForgedOps shall never create work simply to document work.

Every click, field, acknowledgement, approval, workflow, escalation, notification, task, or status must justify its existence by solving a real operational problem.

If a feature creates more friction than value, it fails regardless of technical elegance.

---

### Rule 1 — Work Over Clicks

The platform tracks work. The platform does not track meaningless clicks.

Bad: Click Acknowledge · Click Read · Click Confirm · Click Understood · Click Seen
Good: Correct deficiency · Submit revision · Close incident · Complete inspection · Resolve variance · Verify correction

If no action is required, no acknowledgement should be required.

### Rule 2 — Information Is Not A Task

Most notifications should be informational.
Examples: Daily Report Submitted · Payroll Batch Created · New Employee Added · Safety Meeting Uploaded.
These should notify people but create zero required actions. Information should not become work.

### Rule 3 — Every Workflow Has One Owner

At any point in time, a workflow must have exactly one accountable owner.
Not: PM · Superintendent · Safety · Payroll · Executive simultaneously.
Instead: Current Owner = John Smith · Role = Project Manager. Everyone else can observe. Only one person owns the next action.

### Rule 4 — Every Workflow Must End

Every workflow requires Open · Active · Resolution · Closure.
No workflow may remain permanently open. No workflow may exist without a defined closure path. No workflow may depend on tribal knowledge to determine completion.

### Rule 5 — Public-Gate Simplicity

Field users should never be forced into complicated systems.
If a Foreman can complete a task through One link · One page · One correction · One submit — that is preferred.
The field experience must remain simpler than email.

### Rule 6 — Minimize Human Decisions

Software should make decisions whenever rules are known. Humans should only make decisions requiring judgment.
Software decides: Routing · Ownership · Notifications · Escalation timing · Due dates · Status progression.
Humans decide: Corrective actions · Approvals · Operational judgments.

### Rule 7 — Accountability Must Be Automatic

Users should not manually create accountability. Accountability should be generated by workflow movement.
Daily Report Returned → automatically assigned. QA/QC Deficiency → automatically assigned. Incident Investigation → automatically assigned.
Ownership should emerge from the workflow itself.

### Rule 8 — Reduce Operational Noise

The platform should not notify everyone.
Notify: the owner · required stakeholders.
Do not notify: entire departments · large groups · people with no required action.
Notification fatigue destroys accountability.

### Rule 9 — Operator First

Whenever there is conflict between Audit Perfection and Operational Practicality, Operational Practicality wins.
Construction is the customer. The platform exists to support operations. Operations do not exist to support the platform.

### Rule 10 — Space Shuttle Backend, Toy Airplane Frontend

The platform may be incredibly sophisticated internally. The user experience must remain Simple · Fast · Obvious · Mobile-friendly · Learnable in minutes. Complexity belongs behind the curtain. Never in front of the user.

## PART II · CONSTITUTIONAL OVERRIDE

### Supremacy clause

If a future audit, recommendation, workflow, ownership model, escalation model, reporting model, accountability model, White-Label design, Customer #2 architecture, Operations Center feature, or ForgedOps platform enhancement conflicts with the ForgedOps Operational Design Constitution, the Constitution prevails **unless explicitly overridden by the operator.**

### New mandatory audit axes (in addition to existing axes)

All future audits must evaluate not only:
* Completeness · Accountability · Ownership · Escalation · Auditability · Compliance

but also:
* **User Friction**
* **Click Burden**
* **Workflow Simplicity**
* **Operational Practicality**
* **Field Adoption Probability**

### Success test

A workflow is only successful when it is:
1. **Operationally Complete**
2. **Operationally Accountable**
3. **Operationally Simple**

Failure of any one criterion constitutes a Constitutional Failure.

A workflow shall not be considered successful merely because it is auditable.

### Anti-checklist clause

The platform must never become an auditor's checklist system.
The platform must remain an operator's execution system.

## PART IV · AMENDMENTS

### AMENDMENT 001 · Evidence Over Acknowledgement (2026-06-02 · operator-issued · verbatim)

#### Core Principle

Evidence of work is always superior to evidence of acknowledgement.

The platform should prefer proving that work occurred rather than proving that a button was clicked.

#### Rule 11 — Evidence Over Acknowledgement

ForgedOps shall never require acknowledgements when objective operational evidence already exists.

The system should capture evidence naturally from work being performed.

The system should avoid creating separate acknowledgement workflows whose only purpose is documenting awareness.

#### Preferred Evidence Hierarchy

##### Tier 1 — Work Performed (Strongest Evidence)
Examples: Daily Report submitted · QA/QC correction completed · Site Inspection deficiency corrected · Payroll variance resolved · Incident investigation completed · Equipment inspection submitted · Safety meeting conducted · Training completed · Production entered · Revision submitted.
These actions are evidence. No acknowledgement required.

##### Tier 2 — Participation Evidence (Acceptable Evidence)
Examples: Safety Meeting attendance · Toolbox Talk attendance · Sign-in roster · Training attendance · QR attendance scan · Device attendance capture.
Participation creates evidence. No additional acknowledgement should be required.

##### Tier 3 — Access Evidence (Conditional Evidence)
Examples: JHP opened · PDF downloaded · Revision viewed · Correction notice opened · Notification consumed.
Access may provide evidence. Additional acknowledgement should only be required when legally necessary.

##### Tier 4 — Acknowledgement Evidence (Weakest Evidence)
Examples: I Agree · I Understand · I Have Read This · I Acknowledge · Confirm.
Acknowledgement alone is not proof of understanding. Acknowledgement alone is not proof of compliance. Acknowledgement alone is not proof of work. ForgedOps should treat acknowledgement as a last resort.

#### Constitutional Test

Before any acknowledgement workflow is approved, the following question must be answered:

> "What operational problem is solved by requiring this acknowledgement?"

If the answer is "None" — the acknowledgement shall not be built.

#### Worked examples (operator-issued)

**JHP Example**
Bad: Open JHP → Click Acknowledge → Click Confirm
Good: Worker accesses JHP → System records User · Project · Timestamp · Device · Version → Evidence automatically captured · no acknowledgement required.

**Daily Report Example**
Bad: Report Returned → Acknowledge Return → Fix Report
Good: Report Returned → Fix Report → Resubmit. The correction itself is evidence.

**Safety Example**
Bad: Attend Meeting → Acknowledge Attendance → Acknowledge Understanding
Good: Attend Meeting → Attendance captured. The attendance is evidence.

#### Executive Standard

ForgedOps shall favor **Evidence of Work** over **Evidence of Clicks** at every layer of the platform. The goal is operational execution. Not acknowledgement collection.
